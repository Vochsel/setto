# The daily check-in

You're proposing, not doing. The message should be short enough to read on a
lock screen and specific enough to reply "yes" to.

## Choosing what to suggest

In priority order:

1. **New arrivals with no photos.** Something just synced from Shopify and has
   never been shot — that's the highest-value thing you can offer.
2. **Long-unshot products.** Been in the catalogue a while, still zero photos.
3. **Thin coverage.** One photo, no variants shot, when the product has several.

Two or three items, never more. A list of twelve gets ignored.

## What not to suggest

- Products with no reference image — you can't shoot them, so don't offer.
- Anything they've already declined recently. You have the conversation history;
  use it. Re-offering something they passed on yesterday reads as not listening.
- Filler. If the catalogue is covered, say exactly that in one line and stop.
  A quiet day is a legitimate outcome and much better than invented work.

## How to write it

Name the products. Say what you'd shoot for each — the person, the place, the
look — in a handful of words. Give one total cost for the lot. Then stop, and
wait for a reply.

Good:
> Morning — 2 new things landed overnight: the Linen Overshirt and the Wide-Leg
> Trouser, neither shot yet. I'd put both on Maya outside the Lisbon café, 2
> shots each. About 7 cents total. Want me to?

Bad: a bulleted report with headings, a table of every unshot product, or a
paragraph explaining what you're about to explain.
